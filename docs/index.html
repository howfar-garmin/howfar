A free data field for Garmin watches

How many times have you wanted to know how long to the next checkpoint?

You asked a volunteer, then got angry at how far off they were. HowFar? puts the answer on your wrist: the time to the next checkpoint of your course, worked out from how you climb and descend, and updated every second.

On your watch

What the field shows

Add HowFar? to one of your activity's data screens, load a course with checkpoints, and run. It shows three lines.

P Rif. Deffeyes
1:18:41
@ 19:12
  1. Top line: the next checkpoint. The name comes from your course. The letter in front tells you how much the watch knows about the trail ahead: P it knows every climb and descent to the checkpoint (you gave it the course profile, see Prepare a race); ~ it only knows how far away and how much higher or lower the checkpoint is.
  2. Big number: time to get there. Hours, minutes and seconds while you keep moving. Time spent stopped at an aid station is not included, because nobody can guess how long you'll stay.
  3. Bottom line: arrival time. The time of day you'll get there, in your watch's 12 or 24 hour format.

Without a course, or between courses, the field shows --. On a screen with many fields it shows just the big number.

How it works

It learns how you move in the mountains

Your pace on every slope

Every 25 m it notes how steep the trail is and how fast you're going, from steep descents to steep climbs. Your past races give it a head start; today's kilometres count more and more as the race goes on.

The trail ahead

If you give it the course profile, it knows every climb and descent to the checkpoint and adds up your pace on each. Without it, it only knows the total height difference, which is rougher on up-and-down trails.

How your day is going

It notices if you're faster or slower than in past races, and if you've slowed down in the last few kilometres. It also corrects for your watch measuring more distance than the course map: switchbacks and GPS wobble add about 15% on the Tor.

Does it work?

Tested on four real races

Each race was replayed and the watch's prediction checked against reality at every moment. "Past races" means the other three, so no race was used to test itself. Lower minutes and a higher "less than 5 min off" are better. Click a row to show it on the demo watch.
  • Race columns and Average: how many minutes off the watch was, on average, whenever the next checkpoint was less than an hour away.
  • Less than 5 min off: out of every 100 times the watch showed a time, how many were less than 5 minutes off.
  • Off by, 2 km before: how far off the watch was when you were 2 km from each checkpoint, the moment you often check whether to eat or drink now.
  • STL25's route had no checkpoints, so it was split into two stretches of 13 km.
What else was tried

The same races were used to test other ways of predicting: classic hiking rules (Naismith, Tobler), ITRA's km-effort, an energy-cost model of running uphill and downhill, a machine-learning model, a correction for slowing down with distance, and a correction every few kilometres based on the last stretch. None did better, so HowFar? doesn't use them.

  • Minetti A.E. et al. (2002), Energy cost of walking and running at extreme uphill and downhill slopes.
  • Naismith's rule, and Tobler's hiking function in a crowd-sourced revalidation.
  • ITRA, km-effort.
  • Pacing decline in a 173 km trail race, PeerJ 2016.
  • Real-time performance prediction in long-distance trail running, Sports 2025.

Install

Put it on your watch

HowFar? works on the Garmin Enduro 3, fēnix 8 and epix Pro (Gen 2). It will be in the Connect IQ Store soon, which will make installing a two-tap job from your phone. Until then, it can be copied to the watch with a USB cable.

1. Copy it to the watch

  1. Download the file for your watch model from the latest release on GitHub, for example HowFar-enduro3.prg for an Enduro 3.
  2. Connect the watch to your computer with its charging cable.
  3. Windows: open File Explorer; the watch appears like a phone. Mac: install the free app OpenMTP and open it (close Garmin Express first if it's running).
  4. Open the folder GARMIN, then Apps, and drag the downloaded file into it.
  5. Unplug the watch. After a few seconds HowFar? is installed.

Good to know: copied this way, HowFar? works fully, but it has no settings, so you can't paste your pace from past races or the course profile. Those come with the Connect IQ Store version.

When it's in the store: connect the watch again and delete HowFar-….prg from the GARMIN → Apps folder, then install it from the Connect IQ app on your phone, so you don't end up with two copies.

2. Add it to a data screen

  1. On the watch, open the activity you race with, for example Trail Run, but don't start it.
  2. Hold the MENU button (middle left) and open the activity's settings, then Data Screens.
  3. Pick a screen, choose the field to replace, then Connect IQ → HowFar?.
  4. Tip: give it a screen with one or two fields, so the checkpoint name and arrival time fit.

3. Race with a course

  1. Load the race course on the watch as usual (Navigate → Courses), ideally one with the checkpoints marked.
  2. Start the activity. HowFar? shows the next checkpoint and counts down.
  3. The first kilometre or so is a rough guess. It settles as it sees how you're moving.
  4. Save the activity at the end: HowFar? remembers what it learned for your next race.

Questions

        
Good to know

      

      

        
Does it need a phone or a signal during the race?
No. Everything happens on the watch. Your phone is only needed before the race, to paste the optional settings.

        
Does it use more battery?
It uses no sensors of its own: it reads the distance, altitude and course information your watch already records, and does a little arithmetic once a second. The watch's GPS and screen use far more.

        
What about time at aid stations?
The countdown is moving time. It can't know if you'll stop for two minutes or twenty, so add your planned stop yourself. After you set off again it picks up where it left off.

        
What if I go off course?
HowFar? uses the distance to the next checkpoint that your watch's navigation gives it. While you're off the course line the estimate is rougher; once you're back on it, it settles again.

        
Why is it off at the start of a race?
It's still learning how you're moving today. Pasting your pace from past races makes the first kilometres much closer.

        
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. The watch keeps what it learns, and the race files you use to prepare stay on your own computer or phone.

        
Which watches does it work on?
Garmin Enduro 3, fēnix 8 (43 and 47 mm, Solar 47 and 51 mm) and epix Pro (Gen 2) 42, 47 and 51 mm. More models can be added.
