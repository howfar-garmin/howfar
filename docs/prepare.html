Race prep

Get HowFar ready for your next race

HowFar learns your pace on every slope as you run. What it can't learn on its own is the course ahead. Give it the course profile, with every climb and descent between checkpoints, and it knows what's coming. The profile also lets it show the points before and after the checkpoint, including points you add. Make it here, then paste it into the Garmin Connect app. It's optional: without it, HowFar still counts down to your watch's checkpoints. Either way, load the race course on the watch and start it as usual: HowFar knows where you are only from the watch's navigation.

  1. Your checkpoints. Add your race's course file, and any extra points you want.
  2. Paste on the watch. Copy the text into HowFar's settings in Garmin Connect.

Everything happens in this browser. Your files aren't uploaded anywhere, and what you save stays on this device. The only exception is the optional “Add huts and villages” button, which asks OpenStreetMap what's around your course.

Step 1

Your checkpoints

Use the race's course file, the same one you load on the watch. HowFar finds its checkpoints and the slope of every stretch between them, so it knows every climb and descent ahead (the P on the watch).

Your watch tells HowFar only about the checkpoints in the course you load. Here you can add more: tops and bottoms of big climbs, huts and villages along the way. HowFar shows them too. The course on your watch, with its alerts and checkpoint list, stays as it is: only HowFar knows the points you add.

Course profile

Course and checkpoints

Big dots: main checkpoints, where you stop or face a cutoff; the big number shows the time to them. Small dots: minor points, landmarks on the way.

Untick a checkpoint only if it isn't part of this race, like another race's finish; the text below updates. Make main the points where you stop or face a cutoff, like aid stations and life bases: the big number and the arrival time are for them. Make minor the landmarks in between, like cols and huts: on a full screen each shows in small print above the checkpoint's name, with its own time, as in Col d'Arp in 0:35. Points you add start as minor. Press Main or Minor to switch a point. If you don't want the big number to show a checkpoint of your course, make it minor: unticked, it would still count as main, because the course on your watch still has it.

Add more points

HowFar shows these too: climbs and huts found for you, or any point of your own, like a spring or a viewpoint. They start as minor points, landmarks shown on the way. Make one main if you'll stop there or it has a cutoff: the big number then shows the time to it. They appear in the list above, where you can switch or untick them.

From OpenStreetMap: named mountain huts, shelters and villages within 200 m of the course. This is the only thing on this page that goes online. It asks OpenStreetMap what's inside a rectangle around your course. Only the rectangle's corners are sent, never your file.
Or click the course chart above to pick the place.
Want your watch itself to know these points too?

HowFar doesn't need this. But if you want your watch's own alerts, checkpoint list and ClimbPro to show the added points, download the course with every ticked checkpoint. Load it on the watch instead of the original: in Garmin Connect on a computer, Training & Planning → Courses → Import.

With some watches, Garmin Connect loses the added points on the way. If they're missing on the watch, copy the file onto it with its cable instead, into the GARMIN → NewFiles folder (on a Mac, with the free app OpenMTP). Unplug the watch: the course appears in Navigate → Courses, with every point.

Detail level

HowFar stores one slope value every 25 to 400 m, as fine as fits in the setting. Garmin Connect's text length limit isn't documented; 4,000 characters works. If the app refuses the paste, choose a lower value.

The text for the setting “Course profile”



















Where do I get the course file?

    

  1. From the race website: most races publish the official GPX, often one per stage.
    2. 

  2. From Garmin Connect: open the course, then Export as GPX or FIT.
    3. 

  3. Better: once the course is in Garmin Connect, export it again as FIT. That file has exactly the checkpoints your watch will show.
    4. 

  4. Best: the same file you load on the watch. If the two files differ a little, HowFar still finds where each checkpoint is on the profile.
    5. 






A race in stages?

Make one profile per stage and save them all. At each life base, paste the next stage's text and load the next course on the watch. Race files often list every checkpoint of the whole race; HowFar keeps only the ones on this stage.


















Step 2


Paste the text on the watch


HowFar's settings are in the Garmin Connect app on your phone (if you installed HowFar from the Connect IQ Store). Made the text on a computer? See below for easy ways to get it there.




    

  1. Open the Garmin Connect app and go to your watch.
    2. 

  2. Open Connect IQ apps (or Activities & apps), then HowFar, then Settings.
    3. 

  3. Paste the text into “Course profile”. Tap Save.
    4. 

  4. Sync the watch. On your next run with that course, the watch shows P next to the checkpoint.
    5. 





Made it on a computer?


Your library stays in the browser where you made it, so the text has to get to where the settings are. Easiest first:


    

  1. Garmin Express, no phone needed. Connect the watch to the computer with its cable and open Garmin Express. Choose your watch, then HowFar and its Settings, and paste the text there.
    2. 

  2. Mac and iPhone. With the same Apple ID on both and Handoff on, press Copy on this page and paste straight into the Garmin Connect app on the iPhone.
    3. 

  3. Any phone. Paste the text into a note, an email or a message to yourself, then copy it from there on the phone.
    4. 

  4. Your whole library. Press Save a backup file below. Open this page on the phone, press Load a backup file, and copy from there.
    5.
